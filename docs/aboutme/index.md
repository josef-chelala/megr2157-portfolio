#About Me
Hello, I am Josef Chelala, and this is my Sophomore Design portfolio. I am a new transfer student at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte studying mechanical engineering.
Currently, I am an engineering student who wants to develop a deep understanding of what I learn and apply that knowledge accurately. Simply having a basic understanding of how physics works is not satisfactory to me. I want to understand not only what happens, but also why it happens and how that knowledge can be used to solve real-world problems. My hope is that engineering will strengthen my understanding of the world while giving me the tools to create practical solutions that can benefit others.
My family is full of problem solvers and engineers, which sparked my interest in engineering at an early age. I was also fortunate to get an early introduction to the field through my high school's engineering program. I took classes in CAD, circuits, and even a engineering design technology course that took two years to complete . I greatly enjoyed each of these classes because they allowed me to combine technical knowledge with hands-on problem solving.
Growing up, mathematics was my favorite subject. As I got older, I also developed a strong interest in physics and the way it can explain how the world actually works. What is so lovely about physics is that as you sink your teeth deeper, the world becomes almost foreign to our everyday lives and I find that thrilling. Combining my interest in mathematics and physics with my enjoyment of designing and building things made engineering feel like a natural path for me.
After exploring the different fields of engineering, I decided to pursue mechanical engineering because of its broad range of applications. I like the idea of being a versatile engineer who can work with different areas of technology rather than being limited to one specific type of problem. Mechanical engineering allows me to combine mathematics, physics, CAD, materials, manufacturing, and hands-on problem solving.
As I continue my education at UNC Charlotte, I hope to strengthen my problem-solving, communication, and technical skills. In the future, I want to focus more heavily on the manufacturing side of mechanical engineering. Through my classes and personal projects, I have discovered that I particularly enjoy the process of turning an idea into a physical product. Working with machinery and CAD software to design, manufacture, test, and improve products is the type of work I could see myself doing every day.
